Mysore Silk Saree: An Independent Buyer's Guide

An independent guide to the Mysore silk saree: GI-tagged heritage, how to spot an original, price facts and where to find official KSIC showrooms.

The Mysore silk saree is one of Karnataka's best-known textiles: a light, lustrous pure-silk saree, often finished with gold zari at the border and pallu. This site is a small, independent guide that explains where Mysore silk comes from, how to tell an original from an imitation, what prices look like, and where to buy genuine pieces.

Why people search for Mysore silk

Many shoppers start with a simple question: what is the famous saree of Karnataka? The answer usually leads to Mysuru. The silk weaving factory there was set up in 1912 by Maharaja Nalvadi Krishnaraja Wodeyar, and since 1980 it has been run by Karnataka Silk Industries Corporation (KSIC), a Government of Karnataka enterprise. "Mysore Silk" is a registered Geographical Indication (GI), and KSIC is its registered proprietor, so KSIC Mysore silk sarees are the reference point for what the name should mean. You may also see the spelling "Maisuri silk saree"; it refers to the same product.

What you will find in this guide

  • About Mysore Silk: the history, the GI tag and the tradition of pure silk with gold zari.
  • Authenticity & Price Guide: KSIC's code and hologram, the Silk Mark label, a simple burn test, and price facts taken from KSIC's own website.
  • Showroom & Where to Buy: how to find official KSIC showrooms and the official KSIC website.
  • Blog: blouse design ideas for Mysore silk sarees and tips for choosing a silk saree for a wedding.

A note on accuracy

Historical facts on this site come from public sources listed at the end of the About page. Prices and showroom details change, so always confirm them on the official KSIC website (www.ksicsilk.com) before you travel or buy.
