The Story of Mysore Silk, the Famous Saree of Karnataka

Mysore silk is a pure mulberry silk fabric from Karnataka, and the Mysore silk saree is its best-known form. Its story runs through two centuries of royal patronage, government enterprise and legal protection.

Royal beginnings

According to the Mysuru district administration, Tipu Sultan established sericulture at Channapatna in 1785, and the silk farm there has worked on silkworm seed and breeding ever since [3]. In 1912, Maharaja Nalvadi Krishnaraja Wodeyar set up a silk weaving factory in Mysore. It began with 10 looms imported from Switzerland, later grew to 138 looms, and at first wove fabric for the royal family and ornamental fabric for the Mysore armed forces [1].

From the Sericulture Department to KSIC

After Independence, the Mysore State Sericulture Department took control of the factory. In 1980 it was handed over to Karnataka Silk Industries Corporation Limited (KSIC), a Government of Karnataka enterprise, and the factory completed 100 years in 2012 [1]. KSIC says it manages the whole chain, from reeling cocoons to weaving finished silk, in its units in Mysore district [1]. Its GI application lists a silk filature at T. Narasipura and the weaving factory on Manandavadi Road, Mysuru [5].

The GI tag

"Mysore Silk" is entered in India's Geographical Indications Registry as application No. 11. KSIC filed it on 22 July 2004, the certificate is dated 28 November 2005, and the registration is valid until 21 July 2034 [4]. KSIC describes itself as the sole proprietor of the name [1]. In simple terms, the GI protects the name "Mysore Silk" so that it should be used only for the product from its registered source.

Pure silk with gold zari

KSIC states that it uses only pure natural silk and 100% pure gold zari [1]. Zari is a metallic thread; KSIC's GI application describes it as about 65% silver and 0.65% gold [5]. The same application describes pure silk sarees with a crepe de chine base, with or without gold zari in the border, body and pallu [5], which is why Mysore silk is often grouped with crepe silk sarees.
